Return to Eden — v1 new-art briefs

These six briefs fill the art gaps named in the signed design’s §5 table. The global colorful/warm direction is already locked by Brick; no new look direction is proposed (EDEN-COMBAT-DIRECTION.md, operator additions 2026-09-17 11:09; STYLE-BIBLE.md status). These are production briefs, not gameplay changes. No asset is implemented until Brick approves its concept in its intended screen composition.

Shared production and approval contract

  • Palette roles: UI uses pure saturated deep-indigo glass: panel #0B0B2E, card #23234F, well

#05051A, edge #2D2D7F; ally identity #18B5B5 / highlight #8EFFFF; enemy identity #FF3B3B / sub-accent #FF8A00; mineral ◆ #A0EFFF; gas ◈ #FF7A1A; stat #FFCC00; role-tag #9FB4C7; rare/luxury #FFD324; disabled #3E3E6A with #C7C7E0 text. These roles come from STYLE-BIBLE.md “LOCKED HEX PALETTE” and must not be recolored per asset.

  • Outline/readability: field assets use the bible’s black outer stroke and ally/enemy rim-light

rules; UI icons and markers use a clean dark outline and enough contrast to read at their runtime size. Do not use atmosphere or a battlefield texture inside a UI icon.

  • Source/provenance: the worker keeps the editable source (SVG for flat geometry; layered/vector

source or lossless PNG for raster art), the final export, dimensions, palette notes, and a provenance record. Generated work keeps the tool/model, prompt, seed or run identifier, and the reviewed concept image. Do not trace third-party copyrighted art; any external font/art license must be verified at source and shipped as required by the bible. Existing Saira Condensed and OFL.txt are reused, not regenerated.

  • Approval: Farnsworth checks the whole intended shot against the bible; then Brick approves the

concept or requests changes. Approval is recorded with date and concept hash. The final file is checked for dimensions, alpha, palette role, provenance, and path before Godot import. The worker shows the live screen with the final asset; the broader local build still waits on Brick’s pre-QA gate. A generated thumbnail without composition context is not approval.

A1 — Spell icons

Purpose and trace: Icons for Orbital Strike and Med-Wave in the mixed Scrap shop and each spell’s own battle cast control. Exact design references: V2-31, V2-35, §2.6, §6.1.

  • Palette roles: Orbital Strike is rare/impact-coded with a contained gold #FFD324 energy core

and enemy-facing ember-orange #FF7A1A accent; Med-Wave uses ally highlight #8EFFFF plus a restrained green/organic healing signal allowed by the sprite-role rules. Both sit on the deep UI well #05051A; neither uses mineral ice-blue as its body and neither mimics a card border.

  • Silhouette: Orbital = compact descending comet/strike mark with a sharp tail and a single clear

impact point; Med-Wave = compact medical wave/crescent with a readable plus or pulse mark. Flat geometric, chunky-slim, black keyline; no character, hero, free-aim reticle, or tiny text inside.

  • Dimensions/alpha: editable SVG viewBox 0 0 128 128; final 64×64 RGBA PNG or stable imported

SVG, with artwork inside a 96×96 safe area. Transparent background outside the mark; no baked panel, glow, or drop shadow (Godot supplies the UI well/hover treatment).

  • Screen zone/composition: in the shop entry, icon at the left of kind/name/description/SCRAP

price; in battle, icon centered in the separate CAST control on the support rail, alongside READY/SPENT and the fixed-target description. Show each at actual shop and battle scale in the concept sheet, together with one card and one belt slot to prove hierarchy.

  • Landing: source under project art source storage; final at res://assets/ui/kit/orbital.png and

res://assets/ui/kit/medwave.png (or .svg if Godot import is stable). The shop/cast data maps by stable IDs orbital and medwave; do not use emoji and drawn art simultaneously in the same state.

  • Provenance/approval: generated or hand-drawn source must include its brief, palette, source

record and concept hash; Brick approval precedes the spell-control implementation.

A2 — Consumable icons

Purpose and trace: Icons for Rally Cry and Frag Pack in the mixed Scrap shop and the four-slot battle belt. Exact design references: V2-31, V2-34, §2.6, §6.1.

  • Palette roles: Rally Cry uses ally teal #18B5B5 as its primary signal with #8EFFFF highlight

and a small gold/cream emphasis for the rally signal; Frag Pack uses ember-orange #FF7A1A with a gold #FFD324 fuse/impact accent. Both remain distinct from the mineral #A0EFFF and gas #FF7A1A currency glyphs: the orange on Frag Pack is item identity, while the gas color remains confined to the ◈ resource treatment. Place both on #05051A wells with dark outlines.

  • Silhouette: Rally Cry = compact broadcast horn/flag plus a single pulse/radiating mark; Frag Pack

= compact rectangular charge with visible safety/fuse cue. Flat, iconic geometry; no realistic fragments, smoke cloud, character, building, or card frame. The two must remain distinguishable at 32 px without labels.

  • Dimensions/alpha: editable SVG viewBox 0 0 128 128; final 64×64 RGBA PNG or stable imported

SVG, artwork inside a 96×96 safe area. Transparent background outside the icon; no baked slot, charge number, panel fill, or screen text.

  • Screen zone/composition: in a shop entry, icon sits beside BELT ITEM, name, description,

charge grant, and SCRAP price. In battle, it occupies one of four fixed belt slots and is shown with the visible number key 1, 2, 3, or 4, item name, and ×N charges. Include full and empty-slot states in the concept sheet; the icon must not visually read as a spell cast control or a sixth card.

  • Landing: final at res://assets/ui/kit/rally.png and res://assets/ui/kit/frag.png (or .svg

if import is stable), keyed to catalogue IDs rally and frag. The belt may show either item in a slot, but the asset itself carries no slot number or charge count.

  • Provenance/approval: worker retains editable source, palette/provenance record and concept hash;

Farnsworth checks the shop row and a populated/empty belt composition; Brick approves before belt chrome or item art is wired. The shipped emoji catalogue values are a zero-art fallback, not a reason to skip review of this drawn set.

A3 — Map backdrop

Purpose and trace: Full-bleed backdrop for the standalone between-battle map scene. Exact design references: V2-30, §2.4, §6.1, plus the locked map rules in STYLE-BIBLE.md.

  • Palette roles: Opaque deep-indigo space base #0B0B2E with restrained darker indigo/nebula

variation; use #2D2D7F only for quiet structural lift, #A0EFFF for the visited/current route trace, #1CC7C7 for friendly/neutral accents, #FF4A4A/#FF7A1A for fight/encounter accents, and #FFD324 for rare/boss/exit emphasis. The backdrop must not look like the dirty battlefield and must leave high-contrast quiet space for opaque panels and markers.

  • Silhouette/texture: Sparse star/nebula field with subtle cloud depth and a clean horizontal or

diagonal journey-space read. No battlefield units, buildings, live combat, map labels, roads, or UI baked into the image. Keep the texture low-frequency behind the diagram; no fog that hides an actionable choice.

  • Dimensions/alpha: final 1920×1080 RGB/RGBA PNG at 16:9; source may be layered/vector or a

generated raster at the same framing. The backdrop is intentionally opaque (no transparent background), with no baked panel alpha, text, node rings, or route lines. If a source uses alpha, flatten it to the approved indigo base for the final file.

  • Screen zone/composition: fills the entire map scene behind the static diagram. The left 55–60%

must hold our ship, next encounter/core preview, sparse route thread, and node markers; the right 40–45% must remain quiet enough for the opaque shop/encounter panels; the top bar and fork band remain legible. The concept sheet must show the complete map screenshot at 1280–1440 desktop width, not only the backdrop crop.

  • Landing: final at res://assets/ui/map_backdrop.png; source and review concept stay in the art

source record. Map logic draws the ship, core, markers, roads, labels, Scrap, shop, and encounter over it; do not paint those into the backdrop.

  • Provenance/approval: generated source retains tool/model, prompt and seed/run identifier; drawn

source retains editable layers. Farnsworth verifies the whole map shot against the bible’s sparse, airy anti-clutter rule, then Brick approves the concept before map.tscn uses it.

A4 — Map marker and icon set

Purpose and trace: Small markers for shop, encounter, road choice, and the four node regions on the map diagram. Exact design references: V2-25, V2-30, §2.3, §2.4, §6.1, and the locked map accent rules in STYLE-BIBLE.md.

  • Palette roles: Friendly/neutral/shop markers use teal #1CC7C7; encounter/fight markers use

ember #FF4A4A with optional #FF7A1A; road/current-route markers use ice-blue #A0EFFF for the visited thread; rare/exit emphasis may use luxury gold #FFD324; unknown/neutral region text uses cool grey #9FB4C7. Marker rings must stay distinct from the panel edge #2D2D7F, mineral diamond, gas glyph, and card borders.

  • Silhouette: Provide a coherent ring-plus-glyph family: storefront/supply for SHOP, compass or

question-mark-free beacon for ENCOUNTER, forked arrow for ROAD, and four compact region glyphs or ring treatments for REAR, FLANK, MIDFIELD, FORWARD. Region marks must communicate data vocabulary, not imply node capture or a traversable node graph. No tiny prose baked into art.

  • Dimensions/alpha: each marker is an editable SVG viewBox 0 0 128 128; final 64×64 RGBA

PNG or stable imported SVG. Transparent background outside the ring/glyph; no baked map backdrop, panel, route line, label, or hover glow. Keep a 96×96 safe area and a single consistent anchor point so Godot can place markers from pos data.

  • Screen zone/composition: markers sit in the left map diagram over A3’s quiet backdrop: our ship,

next core variant, and sparse static route thread remain the dominant composition. Shop and encounter markers are clickable and visually larger/clearer than informational region markers; the three road markers/cards appear only after battles 1–2. The concept sheet must show normal map, fork-due map, and a map with the shop/encounter panels open, proving markers do not clutter the decision surface.

  • Landing: final set at res://assets/ui/map_markers/shop.png, encounter.png, road.png,

rear.png, flank.png, midfield.png, and forward.png (or same names with .svg where stable). The controller binds markers to side/region/pos and marker type; it must not create an extra map node or traversal system.

  • Provenance/approval: worker retains the shared vector source and per-icon exports, dimensions,

palette roles and concept hash. Farnsworth reviews a complete static map shot for spacing and legibility at 1280–1440 px; Brick approves the set in composition before marker import.

A5 — Bunker sprite

Purpose and trace: The one player-placeable, mineral-bought defensive structure on the live field. Exact design references: V2-28, §6.1, §8b, and B2; the frozen reference’s turret definition is the numeric/shape anchor only.

  • Palette roles: Primary structure reads ally/teal #18B5B5 with #8EFFFF energy highlights,

dark outline #000000, and a restrained #A0EFFF mineral-cost association only in the HUD text, not as the body fill. Impact/muzzle accents may use contested gold #FFD700/#FFD324; do not recolor the bunker ember-red or confuse it with an enemy core.

  • Silhouette: Compact anchored bunker with a strong base, clear firing port/muzzle, and a simple

readable turret or weapon housing. It must look static and defensive, not like a mobile unit, an economy node, a barrier wall, or a second core. Flat geometry, chunky read at field scale, thick dark outline; muzzle flash, projectile, HP bar, hit flash, and death fade are Godot effects, not baked into the sprite.

  • Dimensions/alpha: editable SVG or layered source on a 512×512 square; final 128×128 RGBA

PNG or stable imported SVG, with the bunker inside a 112×112 safe area. Transparent background outside the silhouette; no baked floor, contact glow, projectile, HP bar, mineral price, cursor ghost, or legal-zone indicator.

  • Screen zone/composition: shown in the player legal half of the battle field alongside small

combat units, nodes, ally barrier, and ship. The concept sheet must show the normal standing bunker, a placement ghost at a legal hover position, the dedicated BUILD BUNKER control with 60 ◆, and one travelling projectile. It must also show the bunker damaged/removed without becoming a blocking wall. A map/shop thumbnail is optional only if it reuses the same approved silhouette; no belt-item version is designed under E6(a).

  • Landing: final at res://assets/sprites/structures/bunker.png (or .svg if stable). The

gameplay data names the only structure bunker; do not land it as turret, bulwark, extractor, or foundry and do not create a second structure asset.

  • Provenance/approval: retain editable source and any generated prompt/run metadata, with no

third-party art tracing. Farnsworth checks field-scale silhouette and whole-shot composition, including legal ghost and damage/death states; Brick approves before the placement and projectile implementation uses the asset.

A6 — Second enemy core variant

Purpose and trace: A distinct data-selected enemy core for a later encounter, proving the core slot is real rather than a single hard-coded object. Exact design references: V2-26, §6.1, §4, and A4; the current project’s enemy_core.png is variant 1 and remains unchanged.

  • Palette roles: The enemy identity is ember-red #FF3B3B with orange #FF8A00 sub-accent,

deep blood #6A0D0D shadow and a black outer outline. Use rare/exit gold #FFD324 only if the encounter is explicitly finale/exit-coded; do not use ally teal as the core body, mineral ice-blue as an identity fill, or gas orange as a currency-like icon. The variant must remain legible beside the existing enemy core and enemy barrier.

  • Silhouette: Make variant 2 visibly different from enemy_core.png while occupying the same

semantic slot: for example a compact rogue-faction holdout camp with a hardened command mast, shielded bunker mass, or distinct superstructure. It is a damageable, anchored core with name/HP data only; no core AI, attack animation, deployment, phase, movement, retreat, or extra boss system. Preserve the bible’s chunky, outlined sci-fi construction and enemy jagged/organic vocabulary where appropriate without reusing the existing core silhouette.

  • Dimensions/alpha: editable source on a 1024×1024 square; final 256×256 RGBA PNG, with the

variant inside a 224×224 safe area and aspect ratio preserved at runtime. Transparent background outside the core; no baked field, barrier, HP bar, projectile, health value, encounter title, or map route. Runtime may add contact glow, damage flash and HP bar.

  • Screen zone/composition: on the battle field at the far enemy edge, paired with the enemy

barrier and the existing small-scale alien army; its HP is represented by the shared HUD bar. On the map, the same asset appears as the NEXT ENCOUNTER core preview in the left diagram at reduced size, with a separate text name. The concept sheet must show both the battle and map uses beside variant 1 so the visual difference and common slot are unambiguous.

  • Landing: final at res://assets/sprites/structures/enemy_core_rogue.png (stable .png; do not

overwrite enemy_core.png). Encounter data selects enemy_core or enemy_core_rogue through the core-variant slot; art changes do not add core abilities or AI.

  • Provenance/approval: retain editable source, generation/drawing provenance, palette notes and

concept hash. Farnsworth verifies distinct silhouette, shared scale/slot, and full battle/map composition; Brick approves the concept before it is imported or assigned to an encounter.
