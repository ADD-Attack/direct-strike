0. Gate, authority, and scope

This is a design artifact, not a build. PM handoff is held until Brick explicitly signs off this design. The acceptance flow remains: builder freezes a local artifact → Brick reviews the playable build and says approve, send back, or approve QA → only then does Scruffy verify the named hash (CADRE-INTAKE.md §Gates; EDEN-REQUIREMENTS.md §3 gate).

The frozen pre-overhaul HTML artifact is a feel/meta reference only; it is not a battle implementation source (EDEN-REQUIREMENTS.md §1, §2.1; CADRE-INTAKE.md D1b/D2).

Design approval packet

Brick is asked to approve the screen composition, interaction ownership, art pipeline, and the following documented defaults: E4(a) a spell costs nothing extra at cast; E5(a) an encounter upgrade may grant a whole card; E6(a) belt inventory contains consumables/spells only and cannot deploy a structure. These are not silently closed: they remain operator-overridable until sign-off (EDEN-REQUIREMENTS.md §8a, §8b; V2-35, V2-23, V2-34/V2-28).

The scope is desktop-width local Godot delivery at approximately 1280–1440 px, played through http://localhost:8888/. This artifact designs the battle screen, one between-battle map screen, shop, encounter, belt/spells, Scrap presentation, node-region vocabulary, bunker presentation, and the asset pipeline. Mobile, public deployment, a navigable journey map, the old step-sim, heroes, extra factions, and every other REV 6 OUT item remain out (EDEN-REQUIREMENTS.md §4, §7; CADRE-INTAKE.md D1–D4).

2. Surface plan

2.1 Battle screen — one field, one bottom HUD

The battle is a single wide field with no minimap and no mid-battle map overlay (EDEN-REQUIREMENTS.md §2.4, §4, V2-30). Use the locked live-frame composition: approximately the upper 85% is field and the lower 8–15% is a deep-indigo glass HUD (STYLE-BIBLE.md “Combat-frame composition reference”). The layout is anchored, not pixel-fixed, so it remains legible across the requested desktop range.

Field region (upper ~85%):

  1. Far left: our anchored ship/core and ally barrier. Far right: the selected encounter’s enemy core

variant and enemy barrier. Core bars are also in the HUD; the field art is the immediate object.

  1. Left/right environment uses the locked dirty battlefield treatment: cool teal ally side, warm

ember enemy side, neutral/contested center glow, dust/crater depth, small outlined units. The center stays visually quieter behind the action so the units and projectiles read.

  1. Resource-node sprites sit at authored pos values and carry a small region label or tooltip only

when inspected. Node placement is data-driven and fixed for the battle; it never becomes capture, ownership-flip, or a live minimap (V2-25; §2.3; §4).

  1. Bunker, when standing, sits in the player legal zone and reads as a compact static defensive

object. Its shot is a short bright projectile path from muzzle to target, not an instant beam.

HUD region (bottom ~15%, opaque glass):

  • Top HUD line: BATTLE N/4, bracket, side, and compact left/right core HP bars. Minerals ◆

use ice-blue #A0EFFF; gas ◈ uses ember-orange #FF7A1A; Scrap is absent by rule.

  • Main card rail: two rows of three compact card panels in acquisition order. Each panel shows

its key (Q W E / A S D), name, unit art, mineral/gas cost, cooldown sweep, affordability, and per-unit gas-tech indicator. Locked slots remain visible and say LOCKED; lost cards are not silently rebound (V2-06, V2-10, V2-14, V2-23, V2-33).

  • Support rail: a clearly separate support block contains the four belt slots, spell cast

controls, the economy-unit button, bunker/build control, and a compact enemy-income readout. Labels must name input ownership: 1 2 3 4 for belt slots, CAST on spell controls, and a non-card DEPLOY ECONOMY/BUILD BUNKER control. Do not place belt items or spells into card slots or reuse card keys (V2-24, V2-34, V2-35; M3, N4).

  • Focus/disabled states: a hovered card or control lifts its edge and shows the target; an

unaffordable card/control is dimmed with a reason; cooldown is a visible sweep; locked cards, empty belt slots, and spent spells remain legible and inert. No hidden state is required to operate the battle.

2.2 The map screen — one static decision diagram, not a minimap

The map is a separate scene (map.tscn plus controller) that replaces the battle and reward scenes; the screenshot after opening must show only the map at desktop width (V2-30, §2.4, §7). It uses the visual language of the locked FTL-style map board — sparse indigo space, glowing route thread, event glyphs, fog-like depth if desired — as appearance only. It does not add the OUT journey graph, traversal, fog-of-war decisions, pan/zoom, or a live battlefield minimap (§2.4, §4; STYLE-BIBLE map board’s anti-clutter caveat).

Canonical layout:

  1. Top bar (8–10%): RETURN TO EDEN / MAP, battle progress (NEXT: 2/4), current road

commander when known, and SCRAP ### at top-right. Scrap is the only purse shown here (V2-30, V2-36; §2.5).

  1. Left diagram (55–60%): our ship at left, a single NEXT ENCOUNTER node/core variant at

right, and a sparse static route thread. Draw the next encounter’s node markers from its authored node table. Region colors/shape labels distinguish REAR, FLANK, MIDFIELD, and FORWARD; the diagram is a preview, not traversal.

  1. Right column (40–45%): opaque shop panel above encounter panel. Both panels share the

deep-indigo panel/card/well ladder and never become translucent over a battlefield.

  1. Fork band (when due): after battles 1 and 2, three named road cards occupy a clean lower band

or the left diagram’s bottom. Each names commander/archetype and boon. Exactly one click commits the road and starts the next battle; after battle 3 this band is replaced by one CONTINUE TO FINALE control (V2-18, V2-19; §2.2, E3(a)).

Map interaction rules: shop and encounter are each usable once per map open; the road is one pick when present; no additional node navigation or event set exists. Buttons use visible selected, disabled, claimed, and focus states. A map screenshot must not leave a battle HUD behind it.

2.3 Shop — mixed stock, one Scrap purchase

The shop is a panel on the map, not a collection screen. Each open offers 2–3 duplicate-free entries, mixed among card, belt, and spell kinds. Every entry shows kind, name, icon, what it grants, and a printed SCRAP price. Prices follow the documented defaults: belt 30–45, spell 45–60, card 60–120; at least one card per open exceeds the 45-Scrap base win award so unaffordability can be demonstrated (V2-31, §8b, N3/N5).

The purchase button is the only action on an entry. After one purchase, the entire stock reads CLAIMED THIS MAP OPEN, including entries of other kinds. Underfunded entries are visibly dimmed and inert, with no ◆/◈ price. A purchased card names its new key; a belt item names its slot; a spell names its cast control. Lost cards may be bought back. No relics, upgrades, levels, packs, random bundles, sale/trade action, or second currency (V2-31, §4, M8/N3).

2.4 Investigate encounter — one card, explicit outcomes

The encounter panel names one eligible non-starter card, shows the odds and the three possible outcomes (UPGRADED, NOTHING HAPPENS, DIES) before commit, and has SEND ONE CARD plus DECLINE. After send, it resolves once and names exactly one result. UPGRADED immediately gives a card level; NOTHING HAPPENS leaves the card intact; DIES removes it from the deck and marks it lost. The starter pair cannot be sent, and the deck never drops below two. The panel becomes resolved after one result and cannot be replayed during that open (V2-32, V2-33; M5; E2(a); §8b).

2.5 Consumables belt and spells

The belt is a battle-HUD support surface, not a separate screen. It has four fixed slots. Each slot contains one consumable item and its current charge count; a blank slot says EMPTY. The primary activation input is the number row 1 / 2 / 3 / 4, printed on the slot. It is distinct from Q/W/E/A/S/D card keys, the economy button, and spell controls. One activation removes exactly one charge and shows the existing catalogue effect; zero charges are inert and error-free. Keep the catalogue/ledger shape from KIT_DEFS/run.kit, including a maximum of three charges per item id. The belt carries consumables only under E6(a); a kind:build turret/bunker charge is excluded (V2-34, §2.6, §8a E6, §8b N2/N4).

Spells are held outside the four belt slots. Each appears on a separate CAST control with its icon, name, description, and READY/SPENT state. Click or keyboard-focus + activate that control; it must not claim a card key, belt number, or economy button. Use the shipped fixed target convention: offensive AoE at the enemy’s deepest incursion and support/heal at our line. Under E4(a), casting costs nothing beyond consuming the one spell; no Scrap/◆/◈ is charged at cast. Uncast spells ride forward; spent spells do not. The existing catalogue content is the four shipped definitions: Orbital Strike, Med-Wave, Rally Cry, and Frag Pack; the shop may offer the spell entries and belt items according to their kind (V2-35, §2.6, N3, E4(a)).

2.6 Scrap and the three-resource boundary

The map/shop top bar owns the SCRAP balance. On a win, the result surface names the award and adds it once; on loss, no award is added; a retry pays only when it finally wins. Purchases subtract the printed price exactly. New Campaign resets Scrap to 0. The recap lists Scrap earned and spent.

The boundary is absolute: ◆ minerals are battle-local and pay cards/economy unit/bunker only in battle; ◈ gas is battle-local and pays exactly the six per-unit tech axes plus special-card gate; Scrap is run-scoped and shop-only. Scrap never appears in the battle HUD and never pays for a unit, bunker, gas axis, conversion, or cast (V2-14, V2-27, V2-31, V2-36; §2.5; M7/N1/N3/N5).

3. Vocabulary and art direction

3.1 Node vocabulary (single source of truth)

Use one data record per node:

node = {
  side:   "p" | "e",
  region: "rear" | "flank" | "midfield" | "forward",
  pos:    Vector2,
  yield:  { min: int, gas: int }
}

rear means behind that side’s base; flank is off the base-to-base axis; midfield and forward are contested/up-field regions. Each encounter authors its own table. Both sides mirror roles but need not clone coordinates. Every table has at least one rear node per side and at least one node outside both rear pockets. Nodes do not change side or become capturable during battle. The map reads the same table to preview the next encounter (V2-25; §2.3; M1/B0/B1).

3.2 Bunker vocabulary

Call the one structure BUNKER, never turret, extractor, foundry, bulwark, or a generic building system. It is a player-owned, mineral-bought, static shooter: 60 ◆ default, 260 HP, 9 damage, 1.4 shots/s, range 170; one standing at a time, no movement blocking, no enemy-placed bunkers, no building ledger/queue/upgrade. Its dedicated control uses the unit hover/legal-zone placement model; its projectile visibly travels and impacts. The reference’s turret definition is the numeric anchor only; the operator’s cost, travel, enemy damage, and death requirements are new (V2-28, B2, §0, §4, §8b).

3.3 Visual rules by screen

The operator locked the colorful/warm direction; no new global look direction is proposed (EDEN-COMBAT-DIRECTION.md 11:09 art answer; STYLE-BIBLE.md status). Apply the bible’s whole-screen rules:

  • Battle: dirty saturated field; warm sky/earth; teal ally left, ember enemy right, neutral hot

center; small chunky-slim units with thick dark outline, role accents, rim lights, and contact glow.

  • UI/map/shop: pure saturated deep-indigo glass, opaque and visually cutting through field haze;

panel #0B0B2E, card #23234F, well #05051A, edge #2D2D7F; ally border #1CC7C7, enemy border #FF4A4A, rare #E026FF; disabled fill #3E3E6A with light #C7C7E0 text.

  • Roles and resources: minerals ◆ #A0EFFF, gas ◈ #FF7A1A, stats #FFCC00, role tags

#9FB4C7, luxury/rare #FFD324; never turn currency into identity color.

  • Type: embedded Saira Condensed from assets/fonts/, all labels/buttons/headers uppercase,

operable text 600–800 weight, titles 800/900, body 400–600. Use the approved cream shaded fill, black outline, and shadow treatment. Ship OFL.txt; no runtime webfont or network fetch.

  • New screen rules: the map is sparse and airy despite the FTL visual reference; shop and encounter

cards follow the locked shop panel skeleton; belt/spell controls are compact support cards beside the battle card rail, never a second visual hierarchy above cards.

4. Art pipeline — first-class workstream

4.1 Ownership and production

  1. Farnsworth / Project Designer owns the visual brief, style-bible mapping, asset acceptance

checklist, and the operator review packet. The brief may not change gameplay or invent a new look.

  1. PM dispatches an assigned Worker (Fry or Bender) to produce/import the asset. The worker may

use an approved image-generation pass for concept exploration or a vector/raster drawing pass for final production. No art specialist or external network dependency is assumed by the current roster; the PM assigns the lane.

  1. Final art source: use editable SVG for flat geometric icons, markers, and the bunker where

practical; use lossless RGBA PNG for rasterized finals and for the map backdrop/core variant. Generated raster concepts are review references, not automatically shippable source. Keep the source/brief and final export paired, with dimensions, palette, transparent-background rules, prompt/tool provenance when generated, and license/provenance notes.

  1. Godot landing: final runtime files land under res://assets/ with stable names, for example:

assets/ui/map_backdrop.png, assets/ui/map_markers/{shop,encounter,rear,flank,midfield,forward}.png, assets/ui/kit/{orbital,medwave,rally,frag}.png, assets/sprites/structures/bunker.png, and assets/sprites/structures/enemy_core_rogue.png. SVG may remain as the imported Godot source when its rendering is stable; otherwise export PNG and retain the SVG in the art-source folder. Godot .import files are generated by the engine, not hand-authored.

4.2 Approval and landing gate

For each new art type (map, marker set, bunker, core variant, kit icons):

  1. Farnsworth writes a one-page brief tied to the exact locked palette, silhouette, screen zone,

dimensions, and relevant V2 criteria. If the brief proposes a new look direction, stop and obtain Brick’s direction lock before any build. Here the global direction is already locked, but these art types are still gaps under the bible’s “show before build” rule.

  1. Worker produces a concept sheet with the asset in its intended screen composition, not an isolated

thumbnail only. Designer checks the whole shot against the style bible and rejects drift in color, scale, silhouette, clutter, or UI/field separation.

  1. Show the concept sheet to Brick. Record explicit approval, requested changes, or rejection with

date and artifact hash. No builder codes the affected screen/art type before approval.

  1. Worker produces the final source and export. Designer verifies dimensions, alpha, palette role,

source/provenance, file hash, and that it lands at the named res:// path. A generated asset is never approved from prompt text alone.

  1. The worker shows the live Godot screen using the final asset. Brick’s pre-QA gate then reviews the

frozen local build; only after Brick approves QA does Scruffy inspect the frozen hash (CADRE-INTAKE.md D4/§Gates).

This pipeline does not generate or land art during this design pass. The global colorful/warm direction is already locked; the operator must approve the new asset-type references before implementation.

7. Explicit deviations and unresolved calls

7.1 Deviations from REV 6: none to gameplay scope

No acceptance criterion, resource boundary, OUT item, workstream estimate, or operator-authored combat rule is changed here.
